Understanding the code

What’s inside a GS1 DataMatrix?

The small square code on a medicine pack isn’t just a barcode. It carries the product’s identity, batch, serial number and expiry date in a standard format defined by GS1, the global standards body. GS1 Healthcare supports GS1 DataMatrix as the 2D code for GS1 data on healthcare products.

The same data, printed in human-readable form:

(01) 09501101530003 (17) 290131 (10) LT2408A (21) SN0042977 (11) 260115

(01) GTINWhich product this is (GS1 sample number shown)
(17) Expiry dateYYMMDD: 31 January 2029
(10) Batch / lotLinks the pack to its production batch
(21) Serial numberUnique to this one pack
(11) Production dateYYMMDD: 15 January 2026. Optional on packs.
Code (AI)MeaningFormatWhy it matters
(01)GTIN (Global Trade Item Number)14 digits, the last is a check digitIdentifies the product and pack type. The scanner checks the check digit and warns if it’s wrong.
(17)Expiration date6 digits, YYMMDDThe date the scanner uses to work out the shelf life left.
(10)Batch or lot numberUp to 20 letters/numbersNeeded for recalls and traceability. It’s saved with every check.
(21)Serial numberUp to 20 letters/numbersIdentifies the individual pack, used by anti-falsification systems.
(11)Production / manufacture date6 digits, YYMMDDWhen present, lets the scanner calculate the % of shelf life remaining.

Why the world is moving to 2D codes

Regulators use the DataMatrix to fight falsified medicines and improve traceability:

  • European Union: Delegated Regulation (EU) 2016/161 requires a Data Matrix on most prescription medicines, carrying the product code, serial number, batch number and expiry date.
  • United States: under the Drug Supply Chain Security Act (DSCSA), packages carry a product identifier (NDC, serial number, lot and expiry), generally as a 2D data matrix.
  • Many other countries are bringing in similar rules. Check your national medicines regulator for local requirements.

Reading the expiry date correctly

GS1 dates are written YYMMDD, so 290131 means 31 January 2029. Older labels sometimes used “00” for the day to mean “end of the month”. The scanner reads these as the last day of that month.

Since 1 January 2025, GS1 rules for regulated healthcare products require a real day (for example 31, not 00), to remove any doubt about which day a pack expires.

Understanding shelf life

Expiry is a date. Shelf life is a decision.

The ICH stability guideline Q1A(R2) defines a product’s shelf life as the time during which it is expected to stay within its approved specification, provided it is stored under the conditions on its label. The expiry date marks the end of that period. What matters at receiving is how much of it is left.

Example: made 1 Jan 2026 and expires 31 Dec 2028 (about 36 months). Checked on 1 Jan 2027, about 24 months remain, which is ≈ 67% shelf life left.

AcceptableMeets your minimum % and is outside your near-expiry window.
Near expiryExpires within the months you set (for example 6). Accept with care and use first.
Below minimum / ExpiredLess shelf life left than your minimum %, or already past expiry. Don’t accept.

First Expiry, First Out (FEFO)

Good storage and distribution practice, as set out in WHO guidance (TRS 1025, Annex 7) and national guidelines based on it, uses FEFO: stock with the earliest expiry date is issued first. Knowing the exact shelf life left at receiving helps you place stock correctly and reduce expiry waste.

Minimum shelf life at delivery

Many buyers set a minimum remaining shelf life that deliveries must meet, often in supply contracts or tenders. The right number depends on your organisation, the product and how fast you use it. The scanner lets you set your own. It doesn’t impose one.

Shelf life only holds if the product has been stored as labelled (temperature, light, humidity). A pack that has been exposed to the wrong conditions may be unfit before its expiry date. Always check storage conditions and the physical pack too.

Getting started

Is Shelf Life Scanner free?

Yes. It’s free to use with no account and no sign-up.

Do I need to install an app?

No. It runs in your web browser. If you want it one tap away, add it to your home screen:

  • iPhone / iPad (Safari): Share → Add to Home Screen.
  • Android (Chrome): ⋮ menu → Add to Home screen (or Install app).

It then opens full-screen, like an app.

Which phones and browsers work?

Recent versions of Chrome, Edge, Safari and Firefox on Android, iPhone, iPad, Windows and Mac. Camera scanning needs a device with a camera and your permission to use it. On older devices you can still choose a photo, type the code or type the dates.

Does it work without internet?

You need a connection to open the page and load the scanning engine. After that, every check is worked out on your device, and nothing is sent anywhere. Typing dates by hand doesn’t need the scanning engine at all.

Can I use a USB or Bluetooth barcode scanner?

Yes. Most handheld scanners act like a keyboard. Open the scanner page, make sure no text box is selected, and scan. The code is read automatically.

Scanning

Which codes can it read?

GS1 DataMatrix (the square 2D code on medicine packs), GS1-128, GS1 QR codes, and standard EAN-13, EAN-8, UPC-A and UPC-E retail barcodes. Code 39 and ITF are also recognised. Retail barcodes only carry the product number, so you’ll be asked to enter the expiry date.

The camera won’t start. What can I do?
  • Allow camera access when your browser asks. If you said no before, re-enable it in the browser’s site settings.
  • Close other apps that might be using the camera.
  • If you opened the link inside another app (for example a chat app), open it in Chrome or Safari instead.
  • You can always use Photo or type the code instead.
It won’t read my code. Why?
  • Hold the pack 10–15 cm away, keep it steady, and fill the frame with the code.
  • Avoid glare from shiny foil. Tilt the pack slightly.
  • Good light helps a lot.
  • Very small, damaged or poorly printed codes may not read. Use Type and enter the dates from the label instead.
What if the pack has no 2D code?

Switch to Type. Scan the ordinary barcode to fill the product (optional), then type the expiry date in the day, month and year boxes. Add the manufacture date if you want the % shelf life left.

What does “check digit fails” mean?

The last digit of every GTIN is calculated from the others. If it doesn’t match, the number was misread, mistyped or misprinted. Re-scan, and treat the pack with caution if it still fails.

Dates & results

How is “% shelf life left” worked out?

(expiry − today) ÷ (expiry − manufacture) × 100. It needs both dates, so it only shows when the code has a production date (AI 11) or you type a manufacture date.

How does it decide Acceptable, Near expiry or Below minimum?
  • Expired: the expiry date has passed.
  • Below minimum shelf life: the % left is lower than your minimum (default 60%).
  • Near expiry: it expires within your warning window (default 6 months).
  • Acceptable: none of the above.

Change these in Settings → Receiving rules to match your SOP.

The label only says “EXP 11/2027”. Which day is that?

A month-only expiry is usually taken to mean the last day of that month, and that’s how the scanner reads it when you leave the day box empty. If your organisation or the manufacturer uses a different convention, type the exact day.

What about codes that use “00” as the day?

Older GS1 codes may show a day of “00”, meaning the end of the month. The scanner reads it as the last day of that month. Since 1 January 2025, GS1 rules for regulated healthcare products require a real day instead.

Why does a shiny new delivery show “Near expiry”?

Near expiry is based only on time left, not on how new the delivery is. Short-dated stock is common for some products. Check your warning window in Settings, and decide according to your SOP and supplier agreement.

Your data

Where is my history stored?

In your browser’s storage on the device you used. It’s not uploaded and not synced between devices. If you clear your browser data, use a private window, or switch browsers, your history won’t be there. Export a CSV regularly if you need to keep records.

Can my team share one history?

Not yet. Each device keeps its own history. Each person can export a CSV and combine them in a spreadsheet.

How do I import my product list?

Open Settings → Products → Import CSV. The file needs a header row with a GTIN (or Barcode) column and a Description (or Product name) column. Pack and Item code columns are optional. Names you type for unknown packs are remembered too.

How do I delete everything?

Use History → Clear all to remove your checks. To remove everything, including products and settings, clear this site’s data in your browser settings.

Safety & compliance

Can it tell if a medicine is falsified or counterfeit?

No. The scanner only reads what is printed in the code. Confirming that a pack is genuine requires checking its serial number against an official verification system (such as the EU’s medicines verification system) or other checks required by your regulator. A correctly formatted code doesn’t prove a pack is genuine.

Is this a medical device or regulator-approved?

No. It’s a general decision-support tool for checking dates. It is not a medical device, isn’t approved by any regulator, and doesn’t replace your organisation’s procedures or professional judgement.

Is Shelf Life Scanner made by GS1?

No. It reads codes that follow GS1 standards, but it is an independent tool made by Lumetis Pharma. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by GS1.
